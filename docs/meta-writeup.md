# SafeGuard: Meta "Bringing People Closer Together with AI" write-up (draft)

## Who it's for

Kids aged roughly 8 to 13 who are starting to use Discord and Instagram DMs, and the parents who want them to make friends online safely. Most parental controls leave families two bad choices: block everything, or read everything. Kids lose real friendships in the first case and lose trust in the second.

## How it strengthens connection

- **Kids can make new friends safely, not just be blocked from them.** When someone new messages a child, AI vetting reads the first messages and profile. It gives the parent a short summary, the evidence it relied on, and a recommendation (approve, watch, or block). It also suggests a friendly question to ask the child, such as "How do you know Sam?". The classmate from robotics club gets approved in seconds. The stranger asking for secrets does not.
- **Parents and kids talk more, not less.** The weekly digest celebrates healthy friendships alongside any concerns, and ends with two open-ended conversation starters. Privacy mode means parents only see what matters, never everyday chats.
- **Kids learn instead of being silenced.** Hidden messages come with a short, kind tip ("Safe friends don't ask you to keep secrets from your parents."). Before a child sends their address or phone number, they get a gentle nudge. Swear words are masked, not the whole message, so normal conversation keeps flowing.

## Why AI is essential

Grooming almost never looks harmful one message at a time. "You're so mature for your age", "what school do you go to?", and "don't tell your mom we talk" all pass a keyword filter. SafeGuard's thread analyzer reads the whole conversation and names the pattern building across it (flattery, then personal questions, then secrecy). It cites the exact messages as evidence and explains the pattern in plain language. The same reasoning powers contact vetting and the digest. OpenAI's multimodal moderation checks every image and profile picture for explicit or violent content, which the original homemade model could not do reliably.

## Demo storyline (2 to 3 minutes)

1. A classmate DMs the child. Vetting recommends approve. The parent taps Approve, and the chat appears for the child.
2. A stranger DMs. Their messages stay hidden, and vetting recommends block, citing evidence.
3. In an approved chat, flattery, then "what school do you go to?", then "keep this between us". The risk meter climbs to high, the parent gets a live alert, and the child sees a coaching tip.
4. A swear word shows up masked. An explicit image shows up blurred for the child and reviewable by the parent.
5. The child types their phone number and sees a gentle warning before sending.
6. The parent generates the weekly digest, with its conversation starters.
